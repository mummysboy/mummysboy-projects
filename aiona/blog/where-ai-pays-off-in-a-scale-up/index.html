Where AI actually pays off in a scale-up

Most scale-ups do not have an AI problem. They have a prioritisation problem. Here is the filter I use to find the work where AI earns its keep.

Iona Hirsch13 October 2026 · 5 min read

Every leadership team I speak to has the same list: a handful of tools someone has trialled, a few enthusiastic people using chat assistants on their own, and a vague sense that the company should be further along. What is usually missing is not ambition or budget. It is a clear answer to a simple question: which work, specifically, should AI be doing here?

Start with the work, not the tool

Most teams start from a tool they have seen demoed and go looking for somewhere to use it. That gets the order backwards. Start instead from the work itself. What does each team do every week? How long does it take, how often does it happen, and what does a good result look like?

An hour spent listing the recurring tasks of one team will tell you more than a month of vendor calls.

Four signs a task is a good fit

  • It happens often. Volume turns small time savings into real ones. A task done twice a year is rarely worth automating; one done fifty times a day almost always is.
  • The inputs are text, documents or data you already hold. Emails, tickets, contracts, call notes and spreadsheets are where today’s models are strongest.
  • There is a clear definition of good. If two people on the team would grade the same output the same way, you can measure whether AI is doing it well.
  • Mistakes are cheap to catch. A person reviews the draft before it goes out, or an error is easy to spot and reverse.

Where it tends to pay off first

The details differ by company, but the same areas come up again and again:

  • Customer support: drafting replies, routing tickets, and summarising long threads for a handover.
  • Sales: account research before a call, first drafts of follow-ups, and keeping the CRM current.
  • Operations and finance: pulling the right fields out of invoices, contracts and forms, and reconciling them across systems.
  • Internal knowledge: answering the questions every new hire asks in their first month.

Where to be careful

Be wary of work where a wrong answer is both costly and hard to spot: commitments to customers, pricing, anything regulated. AI can still help there, but as a first draft for an expert, not as a replacement for one.

A simple way to rank the list

For each candidate task, estimate three things:

  1. How many hours a month the team spends on it.
  2. What share of that AI could reasonably take on.
  3. How easy it is to catch a mistake before it matters.

Multiply the first two, discount by the third, and sort. Then pick the highest-scoring task that a single team owns from start to finish. One owner, one workflow, one number to beat. That is your first project.
