21 Local Service Side Hustles You Can Start This Week

August 4, 2026 · 8 min read

No inventory, no website, no startup capital. Twenty-one services built on things you can probably already do — and the honest version of what each one takes.

The short answer: the fastest side income comes from selling a service, not a product. There's nothing to buy, nothing to ship, and nothing to warehouse — you're selling time and skill you already have. The only real question is which one, and how quickly you can get in front of someone who needs it.

What makes a service side hustle actually work

Before the list, three filters. A good candidate passes all three:

  1. You could do it this week. Not after a course, a certification or $400 of equipment. If it needs a six-week runway, it's a plan, not a side hustle.
  2. Someone nearby already pays for it. You're not creating demand, you're intercepting it. Anything people currently hire out is safer than anything you'd have to explain.
  3. It's repeatable. A one-off job pays once. A job with a natural rhythm — weekly, monthly, seasonally — turns one good customer into a year of work.

Home & hands-on

The bread and butter of local services. High demand, low barrier, and customers who rebook.

  • 1. House cleaning. The most reliably rebooked service on this list. Start with your own supplies; standard, deep, and move-out cleans price very differently.
  • 2. Furniture assembly. Flat-pack is a permanent tax on modern life. One screwdriver set and patience.
  • 3. Handyman & small repairs. Mounting a TV, hanging shelves, fixing a running toilet. Be clear about what you won't touch — anything licensed.
  • 4. Interior painting. A single room is a weekend's work and an easy before/after photo for your profile.
  • 5. Yard work & garden clean-up. Seasonal spikes are your friend — leaf clearing and spring tidy-ups sell themselves.
  • 6. Moving & heavy lifting help. If you're strong and reliable, this books on short notice at short-notice prices.

Vehicles & outdoors

  • 7. Mobile car detailing. You go to them, which is the whole selling point. Bucket, vacuum, decent products.
  • 8. Bike repair & tune-ups. Underserved almost everywhere. Brake and gear adjustment covers most of the demand.
  • 9. Pressure washing. Driveways, decks, patios, siding. The most visually satisfying job here, which makes the photos do your marketing.

People & pets

Trust-heavy work — slower to start, stickier once you're in. Reviews matter more here than anywhere else on the list.

  • 10. Dog walking. The classic recurring service. One weekday regular is a standing income line.
  • 11. Pet sitting & drop-in visits. Holiday periods book out weeks ahead — plan around the calendar.
  • 12. Babysitting. Check what your area requires before advertising; certifications are a genuine selling point here.
  • 13. Senior companionship & errand help. Grocery runs, appointments, company. Quietly one of the most in-demand and least-listed services there is.

Knowledge & lessons

The highest hourly rates on this list, and the least physical work. If you can explain something clearly, you can charge for it.

  • 14. Academic tutoring. Math and science pay the most. Exam season is your peak; start before it.
  • 15. Music lessons. Beginners are the whole market — you don't need to be a virtuoso, you need to be two years ahead and patient.
  • 16. Language conversation practice. Fluency is the qualification. Gig's search and messaging both work across ten languages, which matters here more than anywhere.
  • 17. Fitness or swim coaching. Check what your area requires for certification, then sell the outcome rather than the session.

Creative & tech

  • 18. Portrait & event photography. Graduations, engagements, small events. The camera you have is good enough to start.
  • 19. Video editing. Location-independent, which means your local listing can serve customers well beyond it.
  • 20. Social content for local businesses. Small shops know they should post and don't have time. Weekly retainers are normal here.
  • 21. Tech help for beginners. Phone setup, printer wrangling, photo backup, "why is it doing this". Endless demand, almost no competition.

How to launch one this week

Pick one. You can add the others later to the same profile — testing two or three at once is a perfectly good strategy, and switching a listing off is one tap when you're busy.

  1. Download Gig and sign in. It's free.
  2. Write the listing the way a customer would say it. "Deep clean, move-out ready, own supplies, weekends" beats "cleaning services" every time — search reads plain language, so specific text is what gets you found.
  3. Set a price you'd be happy to repeat. If you're unsure where to land, we wrote a step-by-step guide to pricing.
  4. Publish. You're discoverable to people searching near you, and you can add a second listing in another minute.

What you actually keep

Here's the part that decides whether a side hustle is worth the Saturday. Most services marketplaces charge a commission — a cut of every completed job — and some charge you per lead whether it books or not.

Gig charges 0% commission and never touches the payment. You and the customer settle directly, so what you charge is what you keep.

A 20% take rate on ten $150 jobs a month is $300 gone — not for tools, not for advertising, but for being listed. On Gig there's no in-app checkout, no wallet and no payout waiting to clear: customers pay you by whatever you agree, commonly Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, PayPal or cash. Frequently asked

What's the easiest side hustle to start with no money?

A service you can already perform with things you own — cleaning, dog walking, furniture assembly, tutoring or errand running. Services need no inventory, no website and no startup capital, because you're selling time and skill rather than a product. Listing one on Gig is free and takes about a minute.

Do I need a business license?

It varies by city, state and trade — some work (electrical, plumbing, childcare, food handling) is licensed almost everywhere, while informal help like dog walking or furniture assembly usually isn't. Check your local rules for your specific trade before you advertise. You don't need any license to create a Gig profile.

How much of what I charge do I keep?

On Gig, all of it. Gig charges 0% commission and never touches the payment — the customer pays you directly by whatever method you agree, so there's no platform cut and no payout delay. Most services marketplaces take 15–30% of every job instead.

Can I offer more than one service?

Yes. One profile holds several listings, so you can test a few ideas at once and keep whichever gets booked. Each listing can be switched off in a single tap when you're busy, and switched back on later.
