How to Find Local Help Near You (Without Paying Booking Fees)

August 4, 2026 · 7 min read

Finding someone good nearby is mostly a search problem and a judgment problem. Here's how to solve both in a few minutes — and why the fee sitting on top of your quote is worth paying attention to.

The short answer: describe the job the way you'd say it out loud, then judge the results on four things — rating, number of reviews, distance, and how fast they reply. Message before you commit, agree the price in writing, and pay the person directly. On Gig that whole path is free, and there's no booking fee added to what you pay.

Most people start by typing a category — "cleaner", "handyman" — and then wade through a list sorted by something other than how well it fits. That's backwards. The job in your head has details in it, and those details are what actually narrow the field.

First, know what each option costs you

The quote you're shown is rarely the whole picture. Different platforms make money in different places, and it's worth knowing where, because it eventually lands on your bill:

Where people find local help, what it costs the customer, and the catch in each case.
Where you look What it costs you The catch
Quote / lead platforms Usually free to you The pro pays for your contact details, whether or not you book — that cost tends to end up inside their quote
Big booking marketplaces ~10–20% service or booking fee on top Often charged again on repeat bookings with someone you already know
Neighborhood social groups Free No ratings, no history, no way to tell a pro from a stranger with a van
Gig No booking fee, no service fee You arrange payment with the provider yourself — the app never handles the money

Ranges above are typical published rates for the category, not exact figures for any one company. The pattern holds regardless: someone is paying for the introduction, and if it isn't visible on your invoice it's usually inside the price.

Step 1 — Say what you need, not what category it is

The single biggest improvement you can make is to stop typing nouns. Gig's search reads plain language, so the sentence in your head is a better query than the category it belongs to:

  • Weak: "cleaning" — strong: "deep clean for a two-bedroom apartment before a move-out inspection"
  • Weak: "tutor" — strong: "algebra tutor for a nervous 15-year-old, weekday evenings"
  • Weak: "handyman" — strong: "hang three heavy mirrors on a plaster wall"

The specific version filters out most of the mismatches before you read a single profile. It also works in any of ten languages — you can search in the language you think in, and message someone who replies in theirs.

Step 2 — Read the four signals that actually predict a good job

Once you have results, most of the profile is noise. These four aren't:

  1. Rating and review count together. A lone 5.0 tells you almost nothing; 4.8 across thirty jobs tells you a lot. Gig weights ratings by confidence for exactly this reason, so a single glowing review doesn't leapfrog an established record.
  2. Distance. Travel time is a real cost, and it shows up as either a higher price or a later slot. Closer is usually cheaper and sooner.
  3. Response time. Someone who typically replies in minutes will usually also turn up when they said they would. It's the cheapest reliability signal you get.
  4. How specific their listing is. "Cleaning services" is a person who wants any job. "Move-out deep cleans, same-week, own supplies" is a person who has done yours before.

Step 3 — Message before you commit

A two-minute conversation prevents most bad bookings. Ask the three questions that surface problems early:

  • "Have you done this exact job before?"
  • "What's included, and what would make the price change?"
  • "When could you realistically start?"

On Gig this happens in the app, so you don't hand over your phone number or email to find out someone's unavailable. Messages auto-translate too, which quietly widens the pool of people who can actually help you.

Step 4 — Agree the price, then pay them directly

Get the number and the scope in the chat before work starts. Not for legal reasons — just because "$120, includes materials, Saturday morning" written down is a different conversation to the same words remembered differently a week later.

Then pay the person, not a platform. Gig has no in-app checkout, no wallet, and no escrow. Providers can list the handles they prefer — Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, PayPal — and you settle it between you.

No booking fee, no service fee, no payment sitting in a platform's account for a week. You and the person you hired agree a number, and that's the number.

That's also why the provider's side is 0% commission: an app that never touches the payment can't take a slice of it. If you're curious how a free, commission-free app pays for itself, we explain the model here.

A note on judgment: paying directly means the usual common sense applies. Agree the price up front, don't pay in full before any work has happened, and use the in-app chat so the conversation is on the record. Gig is also newer than the big platforms, so how many providers you'll find genuinely varies by area — it's worth a look, not a guarantee.
